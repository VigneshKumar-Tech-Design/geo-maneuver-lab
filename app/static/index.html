Geostationary vs geosynchronous

A geosynchronous orbit has a period of one sidereal day (a ≈ 42,164 km). A geostationary orbit is the special case that is also circular and equatorial, so the satellite holds one point on the equator. Tilt it and the ground track becomes a figure-eight; make it eccentric and the track swings east–west.

The satellite is placed over the slot longitude at the epoch (exact when inclination is ~0). True-anomaly input only.

Maneuvers

R, T, N apply only to “custom”. RTN: R radial, T along-track, N cross-track.
